## Why SEO is a system

Google describes SEO as helping search engines understand content and helping users find a site and make decisions from search results. That means a page can be technically crawlable yet commercially weak if it does not satisfy the searcher’s intent.

## Start with search intent

Build topic clusters around what customers actually need: service, problem, comparison, location, price guidance, process and trust questions. A digital marketing agency might therefore build separate service pages, location pages and educational articles instead of placing every keyword on the homepage.

## Technical foundations matter

Use descriptive titles, useful meta descriptions, semantic HTML, crawlable links, canonical URLs, XML sitemap, robots rules where appropriate, fast pages and mobile-friendly layouts. Google’s developer guidance explicitly recommends descriptive titles, semantic HTML and accessible text content.

## Content should be people-first

Google’s current guidance emphasizes helpful, reliable, people-first content and warns against producing large amounts of search-engine-first content simply to attract traffic. That supports a smaller set of stronger articles over a large archive of repetitive pages.

## Build authority through connected topics

Internal links should connect related service pages, guides and case/portfolio material. External sources can support factual claims. Over time, the site can establish topical depth around its actual commercial expertise rather than chasing unrelated keywords.

## Measure SEO commercially

Search Console shows search visibility signals; analytics can show engagement; a lead system shows whether organic visitors become enquiries. Rankings alone are not the business objective. The useful question is which organic topics produce relevant users and qualified demand.
