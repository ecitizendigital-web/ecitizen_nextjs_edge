## The homepage is not the whole SEO strategy

The homepage establishes the entity and proposition, but service pages usually target more specific intent. A business website should therefore have clear service architecture instead of placing every keyword and offer on one page.

## Make the first screen answer three questions

What do you do? Who is it for? What should I do next? A visitor should be able to understand the offer without hunting through decorative sections. Clear headings and semantic structure also help search engines understand the page.

## Create multiple conversion routes

Different users prefer different actions. A phone number, WhatsApp route, contact form and relevant service CTA can coexist when the hierarchy is clear. Track each route separately so the business can learn which channels produce qualified enquiries.

## Performance is part of the experience

Google’s page-experience guidance emphasizes Core Web Vitals, secure delivery, mobile display and avoiding intrusive elements. Performance is therefore both a UX concern and part of a modern search strategy.

## Trust should be specific

Real business information, transparent service descriptions, process explanations, genuine portfolio work and accurate contact details create stronger trust than generic claims. Do not use fabricated client logos, testimonials or performance statistics.

## SEO and conversion should share the same information architecture

The best structure lets search visitors land directly on the page that answers their question and then gives them a relevant next step. This reduces the distance between query, understanding and enquiry.
