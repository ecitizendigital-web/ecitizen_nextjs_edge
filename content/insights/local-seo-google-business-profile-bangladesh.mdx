## Local search starts with accurate business identity

Business name, address, phone, hours, website and category should be consistent. The goal is not to add as many location keywords as possible; it is to make the business easy to understand and contact.

## Google Business Profile and the website have different roles

A Business Profile helps customers discover a business in Google surfaces such as Search and Maps. The website provides deeper information: services, pricing context, FAQs, location/service areas, team information and conversion paths.

## Use LocalBusiness structured data carefully

Google’s LocalBusiness documentation explains how structured data can communicate business details such as hours and other attributes. It should reflect real information on the page and be validated before deployment.

## Location pages need substance

A location page should answer why the business serves that place, what services are available, how to contact the business and what relevant local information matters. Copying the same page across many cities with only the city name changed creates little user value.

## Reviews are trust signals, not SEO decorations

Businesses should collect genuine customer feedback through legitimate processes and never manufacture reviews. Testimonials, ratings or client claims on the website should be real and attributable where appropriate.

## Measure local discovery

Track branded vs non-branded search queries, calls, website clicks, direction-related actions where available, WhatsApp enquiries and contact-form submissions. The objective is to connect local visibility to actual business actions.
